# A common vocabulary for solar fronts

Name the observed feature first, then state the physical interpretation and its evidence.
Imaging terms, MHD wave families and driver descriptions answer different questions.

| Term | Meaning | What the name does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Solar front | A tracked boundary or propagating pattern in solar observations | A specific MHD family or a material surface |
| EUV front / WL front | A front identified in extreme-ultraviolet / white-light observations | Fast versus slow, shock versus rarefaction, or a common three-dimensional surface |
| EIT wave | A historical observational term used in the solar literature | A unique MHD mode from the name alone |
| Dimming | A decrease in measured brightness | A rarefaction without additional state, thermal and geometrical checks |
| Fast / slow magnetosonic wave | An MHD characteristic family defined by the plasma state and magnetic geometry | Shock versus rarefaction from the family name alone |
| Shock | An admissible discontinuous compression with entropy production | Fast versus slow from speed or brightness alone |
| Rarefaction | A continuous expansion along an appropriate wave curve; isentropic in the ideal adiabatic model | Every dark region behind a front |
| Alfvénic rotation | A field/velocity rotation satisfying the ideal-model invariants and jump conditions | An additional bright ridge or unique identification from one correlation |
| Contact | A material discontinuity; its allowed jumps depend on the normal magnetic field | Automatic equivalence with a visible CME edge |
| Riemann fan | The connected wave pattern solving specified initial LEFT/RIGHT states | Seven nonzero waves for every input, or a global description of every eruption |
| Piston-driven / blast-like | Descriptions of how a disturbance is driven and evolves | A separate MHD characteristic family |

Report the measured image-pattern speed separately from plasma velocity and front-normal speed.
An EUV and a WL front should be associated using geometry and timing before their measurements are combined.
For a single discontinuity, use immediately adjacent states. The original LEFT/RIGHT states define the whole fan.

Example wording: "An EUV front is observed. Under the stated plasma and magnetic constraints,
its adjacent states admit a fast-shock connection." State whether competing connections are
excluded, remain allowed, or have not been searched completely.

For fixed initial states, geometry and equations, the intermediate states are coupled by
conservation and wave-curve conditions. In a regular nondegenerate fan the family order is
fast−, Alfvén−, slow−, contact, slow+, Alfvén+, fast+. This is not an arbitrary sequence
of independently chosen states. Zero-amplitude waves can disappear. MHD nonuniqueness,
degeneracy and incomplete observational inputs remain separate issues; a rigid connection
does not itself prove a unique solution.
